# Your AI center of excellence should work itself out of a job

**Author:** Amit Kothari
**Published:** 2025-11-08

## The dissolution argument (verbatim)

"Real success is that the team's knowledge lives everywhere. The people move on to other roles,
but the capability stays."

The author advocates an **18-month timeline** with "an end date from day one."

## Drucker reference (verbatim)

"The best organizations push capability to where the work happens, not keep it locked in a
central group" — paraphrasing Drucker's *The Effective Executive*.

## Statistics (verbatim)

- "Very few organizations qualify as AI high performers seeing real bottom-line impact"
- "Two-thirds of workers say their organization has not been proactive in AI training and
  upskilling"
- "95 percent of generative AI pilots at companies failing to achieve rapid revenue acceleration"
- "58% use AI at work and 57% hide it"

## Core argument

CoEs function as **temporary scaffolding, not permanent foundations**. Success means making the
team unnecessary by embedding AI knowledge throughout the organization via rotations,
peer-to-peer learning, and documented frameworks that business teams own independently.

## Notes on the playbook's use of this source

permanent gatekeeper has failed its mission" — both accurate.

The playbook **omits the concrete part**: the 18-month timeline with an end date from day one.
Section 12.2's "Make the CoE invisible over time" keeps the sentiment and drops the deadline,
which is the only thing that makes the sentiment operable.
