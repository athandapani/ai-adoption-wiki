# Your Resistant Employees Know Why Your AI Adoption Is Failing

**Author:** Ajay Pundhir (Forbes Tech Council)
**Published:** 2026-02-05

## Statistics (verbatim)

- "95% of enterprise AI pilots are failing to deliver measurable business returns" (MIT NANDA
  initiative)
- "31% of employees admit to actively undermining their company's AI rollout" (Writer survey)
- "76% of executives believe their employees feel enthusiastic about AI adoption. The reality?
  Just 31%" (BCG/Columbia Business School)
- "42% of C-suite executives report that AI adoption is tearing their company apart" (Writer
  survey)

## Four types of resistance

- **Tool resistance** — technology limitations
- **Strategy resistance** — deployment misalignment
- **Trust resistance** — credibility issues
- **Capability resistance** — insufficient training

## Core argument

Rather than crushing skepticism, organizations should decode what resistance signals. Companies
that empower domain experts to surface problems outperform those imposing solutions top-down.

## ⚠ The number the playbook needed and didn't use

The playbook cites this source once, in Section 8.4, for a single clause: "treat resistance as
signal, not noise".

It does not mention **"95% of enterprise AI pilots are failing to deliver measurable business
returns"** (MIT NANDA) — despite that figure appearing here *and* in ref 43. Two of the
playbook's own sources carry it, and it appears nowhere in the playbook. Section 1.1 instead
reports "85% saying AI has exceeded expectations".

Nor does it mention the executive-perception gap: **76% of executives think employees are
enthusiastic; 31% actually are.** That directly undercuts Section 1.3's conclusion that
"the barriers are structural and resource-based, not attitudinal" — a conclusion the playbook
draws from OECD's 6–7% resistance figures and then builds an entire intervention model on.
