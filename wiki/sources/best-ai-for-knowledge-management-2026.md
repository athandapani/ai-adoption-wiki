## Body

**Author:** Craig Hunt, Fractional CTO at Sagecrest Solutions.

**The diagnosis** — the best single sentence in this corpus on why knowledge management has always
failed:

> "knowledge management failed at most organizations through 2025 because **the cost of capturing
> knowledge exceeded the value to the capturer**."

**Capture before search:** "Knowledge teams that ship search before capture work end up with great
search across a thin knowledge base. Get capture working, accumulate knowledge, then optimize
search."

**Permissioning:** "AI search and synthesis must honor existing access controls. Tools that bypass
permissions for the AI features create compliance gaps that catch teams later."

**Timeline:** "Most platforms ship in 4-12 weeks for initial integration. Maturity takes 6-12 months
as the team adapts workflows."

Three 2026 shifts: automated capture from meetings without manual transcription; AI search returning
answers not document lists; verification workflows flagging stale content.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**The playbook uses this source well** — Section 12.2's critical success factors trace here
faithfully, including the 6–12 month maturity horizon. (It says "8–12 weeks" for initial value where
this says "4–12"; immaterial.)

**But it ignores the permissioning warning in its own architecture.** The
[[three-layer-reference-architecture|three-layer reference architecture]] has no access-control
boundary anywhere — while proposing a Layer 3 "expert finder" over Layer 1 HR exports and Slack.
The playbook lists permission-respect as a critical success factor and then draws a design that
can't honor it.

The capture-cost diagnosis is also the strongest argument *against* the playbook's own thesis, if
AI-assisted capture doesn't actually collapse that cost — a Second Brain then fails for exactly the
reason wikis always failed, whatever compiles it.

Vendor-adjacent: a fractional CTO ranking platforms, where "capture is now cheap" is convenient for
anyone selling automated capture.
