## Body

**Context:** technology industry, 200 employees.

Before/after:
- Search time: "from **25 minutes to 3 minutes** (88% reduction)"
- Query success rate: "soared to **94% from 23%**"
- Developer onboarding: "from **8 weeks to 4 weeks** (50% faster)"
- Support resolution: "from **4 hours to 45 minutes** (81% faster)"

Financials:
- Annual savings: "**$480K** ($300K support cost reduction + $180K productivity gains)"
- Investment: "**$154K**"
- **ROI: "312% within 6 months"**; payback "3.2 months"

This supplies three of the eight rows in the playbook's Section 2.4 business-case table, and the
312% figure it leads with.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**No author. No publication date.** For a source carrying the playbook's headline ROI number, that
is disqualifying on its own.

**n=1, and generalized well past its evidence.** This is one 200-employee *technology* company.
The playbook applies its conclusions to manufacturing, distribution, logistics, professional
services, and healthcare — sectors with different data estates, literacy baselines, and regulatory
constraints. Nothing in the case study supports the extension.

**Survivorship.** Case studies are written about deployments that worked, by the people who built
them. Mavvrik's "30% of GenAI projects abandoned after POC" and "60% see minimal or no value" is the
denominator. No knowledge-system failure case appears anywhere in this corpus.

Note the coincidence worth flagging: this case reports **"$300K support cost reduction"**, and the
playbook's untraceable knowledge-attrition figure is **"$300,000 per departing senior employee"**,
attributed to a different source (ref [^10]). Possibly unrelated. Possibly a number that migrated.
