## Body

The two figures:

- **"only half offer training to non-technical employees, with that figure growing just 4
  percentage points between 2024 and 2025"**
- "43 percent of employees believing many people will lose jobs to automation within five years"

The 4-point movement is the finding. A year of intense organizational AI attention barely shifted
the training rate — which is the empirical basis for the playbook's argument in Section 3.2 that
[[ai-literacy|AI literacy]] is the neglected lever.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

A news aggregator summarizing a Forrester report. The report isn't named, dated, or linked, and
Forrester research is paywalled — sample, population, and question wording all unknown.

"Offer training" is a weak measure. An org that publishes an optional e-learning module counts.
Nothing here reports uptake, completion, or effect.

The 43%-fear-job-loss figure is uncontextualized: it's an employee-sentiment number sitting in a
report about employer behavior, and the source doesn't connect them. Compare
[[resistant-employees-know-why|ref 37]]'s finding that 76% of executives think employees are
enthusiastic while 31% are — the two together suggest employers are measuring their workforce
badly, which is a different and larger problem than under-training it.
