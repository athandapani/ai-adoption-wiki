## Body

**Authors:** Eric Lundberg (written), Linus Ingemarsson (reviewed).

- **"82% of AI adoption failures trace to change management gaps, not technology"**
- **McKinsey:** "#1 barrier to AI adoption is organizational — change management and silos outrank
  technology gaps"
- **"18% of firms currently use AI in at least one business function (Jan 2026)"**
- "83% of Agile practitioners use AI, but most spend under 10% of work time with it"

**Academy of Management study:** supervisors who co-present AI-generated feedback alongside
employees — rather than delegating the AI interaction — measurably reduce pushback. This is the
sole basis for the playbook's Section 8.3.

Its prescriptions: diagnose resistance pre-deployment via stakeholder mapping and anonymous
surveys; tailor communication (**prevention-focused vs. promotion-focused** language); model from
the top; sequence pilots starting with invisible-workflow tools.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**The 18% figure is a live contradiction the playbook never addresses.** This source says 18% of
firms use AI in at least one business function as of January 2026. The playbook's Section 1.1 says
78–92% of mid-market firms use AI (ref [^2]) and 39% of SMEs (ref [^1]) — and cites *this source*
in Section 8. A 4× spread across three sources in one document.

**The prevention/promotion-focused idea originates here**, not in ref [^27] — but the playbook's
Section 8.2 ADKAR table presents it as part of a framework cited to both, blurring which source
contributed it.

The 82%-of-failures figure has no visible methodology; attributing 82% of anything to one cause
should invite scepticism. The Academy of Management study is unnamed, undated, and unlinked, so its
design and effect size are unknown — for a claim the playbook calls "the single highest-leverage
variable in AI adoption".
