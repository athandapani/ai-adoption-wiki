## Body

Its ADKAR table maps each stage to an AI context, a common stall, a tactic, and a warning sign.
The Desire row's warning sign — **"High shadow-AI usage"** — is the most useful line in the corpus's
change-management material, and connects Sections 4.5 and 8 of the playbook, which otherwise never
speak.

**PwC wage premium:** "Workers with AI skills command roughly a **62% wage premium** per PwC, up
from 57% the prior year."

**Champion network:** peer champions answer the practical "how do I" questions broadcast comms
can't. Microsoft reached "~90% monthly active usage" through role-specific training, gamification,
and peer-to-peer learning "rather than top-down mandates".

Its thesis: adoption succeeds **person-by-person**; "Technology is commoditized; organizational
capacity to adopt well is the durable competitive advantage."

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**The playbook's Section 8.2 table is a rewrite, not a reproduction.** Its stall points and warning
signs differ from this source's, and its Desire row recommends segmenting by "employee regulatory
focus — prevention-focused staff need safety narratives" — an idea from ref [^35], not here. The
playbook cites [^27][^35] jointly without distinguishing which contributed what.

The playbook also drops the shadow-AI warning sign, which is this source's most original
contribution.

Digital Applied is a marketing agency selling AI transformation services. The Microsoft
90%-monthly-active figure is uncited. The PwC premium is real but cross-sectional — see
[[pwc|PwC]] for why it doesn't mean what it's used to mean.
