## Body

**Author:** Lindsey Tishgart.

Corroborating figures:
- "Companies plan to spend **1.7% of revenue** on AI in 2026" — **up from 0.8% in 2025**
- "Companies allocating **≥25% of IT budget to AI expected to rise from 27% → 52%**" (and ≥50%
  rising from 3% → 19%)
- "94% of companies will continue investing even without immediate returns"

Cost discipline:
- "**80-85% of enterprises miss their AI infrastructure forecasts by more than 25%**"
- "Organizations increased AI compute spending by 166% year-over-year in Q2 2025"

Returns:
- "**Less than 1% of executives report ROI of 20% or greater; 53% report only 1-5%**"
- "30% of GenAI projects abandoned after POC"; "60% see minimal or no value"
- "**Nearly 40% of AI time savings are lost to rework**"
- "89% of organizations haven't updated job roles for AI"

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**This source independently verifies two of the playbook's load-bearing numbers** — the 1.7%
benchmark (with its 0.8% base, confirming "more than double") and the 27%→52% shift the playbook
credits to an [[ey|EY survey]] it never cites. That makes it one of the most valuable captures here.

**And the playbook omits everything inconvenient in it.** "Nearly 40% of AI time savings are lost to
rework" directly undercuts the Section 2.4 productivity arithmetic — the $1M–$2M recovered-capacity
claim assumes recovered hours are recovered. "Less than 1% report ROI of 20% or greater" sits against
the 300–500% RAG ROI figures two sections earlier. Neither appears.

Mavvrik sells AI FinOps tooling, and the conclusion is that organizations need AI financial
governance tooling. The 80-85%-miss-forecasts figure is the kind of number a FinOps vendor is
motivated to find.
