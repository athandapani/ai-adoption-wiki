## Body

**Author:** Lior Messinger.

The headline figure, verbatim: "cost businesses **$67.4 billion in losses in 2024** alone reported
in a comprehensive study by **AllAboutAI, 2025**."

On the lawyer case: "When a lawyer submitted a brief filled with fake case citations generated by
AI—and got sanctioned for it". Case not named, no details.

Its argument: enterprises must adopt RAG systems that ground answers in verified organizational
knowledge with transparent source citations.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**The $67.4B figure is third-hand with no derivation.** The chain runs: playbook → korra.ai →
"AllAboutAI, 2025" → unknown method. No sample, no definition of "loss", no methodology at any link.
It's the kind of round, enormous, un-sourceable number that circulates because it's quotable.

**The Air Canada case is not in this source.** The playbook's Section 9.1 attributes to `[^38][^25]`
a sentence covering both the Air Canada chatbot liability and the sanctioned lawyers. Ref [^38]'s
capture explicitly does not mention Air Canada; ref [^25] doesn't surface it either.

Both underlying cases are **real** — *Moffatt v. Air Canada* (2024) and *Mata v. Avianca* (2023) —
so the playbook's claims are true. They're just not supported by the citations attached to them,
which is a different and subtler failure than being wrong.

Korra.ai sells RAG-based answer software; the article's conclusion is to buy RAG-based answer
software. Published August 2025.

Status `revisiting`: captured cleanly, but nothing in it is verifiable.
