## Body

**Methodology (disclosed, which is rare here):** online survey to a panel of 4,800 SMB operators,
February–April 2026; 312 complete responses; stratified to match US Census SMB-by-industry
distribution; $25 incentive; **no vendor sponsorship**.

Median annual AI spend: $34,000 ($5M–$25M), $148,000 ($25M–$100M), **$390,000** ($100M–$200M).
Production adoption rises from 34% ($1M–$5M) to 89% ($100M–$200M).

Budget allocation: customer service 29%, sales automation 19%, content 14%, back-office 13%, voice
agents 9%, **internal knowledge/RAG 8%**, marketing personalization 5%, other 3%.

Outcomes: reduced costs 72%, revenue per employee 58%, customer satisfaction 47% — but **no
measurable impact 18%** and **negative impact 4%**.

Top blockers: **"Don't know which use case" 51%**, no internal AI talent 44%, security 38%, ROI
evaluation 35%, failed pilots 22%, **budget 17%**.

Its conclusion: decision paralysis, not budget, is the primary barrier.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

This is the best-methodology source in the corpus, and the playbook uses it selectively.

**Its blocker ranking contradicts [[oecd|OECD]]'s**, which the playbook builds Section 1.3 on. Cost
ranks *last* here (17%) and *first* there (40%). The playbook reports both and reconciles neither.

**Its spend actuals contradict the [[bcg|BCG]] benchmark** the playbook recommends. 1.7% of $100M
is $1.7M; the observed median at $100M–$200M is $390,000 — roughly 4× lower. Section 6.1 leads with
the benchmark and presents the actuals afterward without noting they disagree.

Population caveat: $1M–$200M revenue, which overlaps but doesn't match the playbook's $50M–$500M
frame. The top band ($100M–$200M) is the only one inside it.
