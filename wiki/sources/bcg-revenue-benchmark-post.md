## Body

- "roughly **1.7% of annual revenues** on AI in 2026, more than double what they spent in 2025"
- "**94%** say they'll continue at that level or higher even if this year's initiatives don't
  deliver a return"
- "meaningful bottom-line impact is showing up for about **6%** of the companies currently using AI"

The post's analysis is the sharpest change-management statement in the corpus, and the playbook
quotes it almost verbatim in Section 8.1:

> "When an AI initiative stalls, it almost never stalls because the LLM model underperformed. It
> stalls because the people who were supposed to change how they work didn't change. Because the
> data feeding the system wasn't trustworthy enough for people to act on. Because the infrastructure
> created constraints that weren't fully planned for. Because the tool added friction or confusion
> instead of removing it, and people quietly went back to what they knew."

Its closing question — not "can we implement the technology" but "is the organization set up to
absorb it" — is the playbook's thesis for Section 8.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

A LinkedIn post, not the BCG report. The report is never fetched, and the playbook calls it "BCG
Radar 6", which is not its name — it's **AI Radar 2026** (see
[[indian-ceos-doubling-down-on-ai|ref 28]]).

The 1.7% figure is well-corroborated (refs [^28] and [^31] agree independently), so the number
survives its weak citation. The **sector split** the playbook attributes to BCG — tech/financial ~2%,
industrial/real estate ~0.8% — does not appear here or in any captured source.

The 94%/6% pair is the most-quoted statistic in the playbook and the least examined. It is equally
readable as measurement failure (the playbook's reading) or as rational conviction in a
general-purpose technology too early to measure. See
[[transformative-integration-gap|The Transformative Integration Gap]].
