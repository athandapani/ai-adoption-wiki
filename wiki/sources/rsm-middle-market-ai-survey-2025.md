## Body

The only source in the corpus measuring the playbook's actual target population — US and Canadian
middle-market firms — rather than "SMEs" generally.

- "78-92% of mid-sized firms are utilizing AI"
- "85% report AI has exceeded their expectations"
- "54% found deployment harder than anticipated"
- "89% plan to increase AI budgets, with 74% focusing on generative AI"
- "67% of organizations acknowledge requiring external assistance for strategy and governance"

Common applications: quality control, customer service, repetitive task automation, productivity.

Takeaway as stated: "Mid-market firms are actively investing in AI and seeing results, but struggle
with scaling. To succeed, blend internal adoption with external support — and back it with
strategic investment."

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**The adoption figure is irreconcilable with the rest of the corpus.** 78–92% here; 39% of SMEs
(ref [^1]); 18% of firms using AI in at least one business function as of January 2026 (ref [^35]).
A 4× spread across sources the playbook cites without noting the conflict. Definitions of "using
AI" differ and none is stated.

"85% exceeded expectations" also sits against MIT NANDA's 95%-of-pilots-fail finding, carried by two
of the playbook's own sources (refs [^37], [^43]) and absent from its text.

**The source is a LinkedIn post about a survey by a consultancy that sells AI advisory**, and the
same post announces RSM's own $1B AI investment. The "67% need external help" figure is published by
the firm selling that help. The survey itself was never fetched and has no URL in the playbook.
