## Body

- "global AI spending forecast to **$2.52 trillion for 2026** (up **44 percent** year-over-year)"
- "**$6.31 trillion** in total IT spending with 13.5 percent growth"
- "Software spending alone: **$1.44 trillion**"; datacenter systems: "$788 billion"
- Software spending growth: "15.1 percent"

The 44% growth figure is what the playbook's Section 3.2 uses to argue AI talent demand is
compressing supply for mid-market buyers.

**Its own framing is the useful part**, and more honest than the playbook's use of it: the Gartner
figures are "**actionable negotiation leverage rather than predictive forecasting**". Its advice —
audit software contracts now, reserve GPU capacity on 18-month commitments, build talent strategy
across career paths, training, and work modes.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

Secondary coverage of paywalled analyst research. No link to a Gartner publication; the forecast
can't be verified against source.

Note the two global totals this vault carries: **$2.52T** here (Gartner, April 2026) and **$2.59T**
in ref [^33] (citing Gartner, May 2026). Probably the same forecast revised a month apart, but the
playbook cites both without reconciling.

The talent-compression inference is the playbook's, not this source's. Rising aggregate AI spend
does not mechanically compress mid-market hiring — and no source here offers salary, time-to-hire,
or offer-decline data that would show it.

Unbylined ("MyBusinessFuture editorial team"), and the piece is written for a DACH mid-market
audience.
