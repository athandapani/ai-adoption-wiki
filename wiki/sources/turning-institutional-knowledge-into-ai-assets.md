## Body

**Author:** Mayank Kukreja, Technical Architect.

The core argument, and the origin of the playbook's Section 2.1 framing: RAG "doesn't require
rebuilding institutional knowledge — it **activates existing documents** already housed in
organizational systems (SharePoint, Confluence, archives) by connecting them to AI models."

The playbook's rendering — "your institutional knowledge is already an asset; the investment is in
activation, not creation" — is faithful and is the strongest strategic claim in the document.

Reported benefit: "Organizations that have implemented RAG-powered internal systems report
meaningful reductions in query resolution time, often in the range of **40 to 60 percent**."

Its stated success factors are notably modest: content governance, retrieval precision, and
auditability "rather than engineering sophistication alone."

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

Netsolutions is a development agency selling RAG implementations, and the article's conclusion is
that you should implement RAG. The 40–60% figure has no methodology, sample, or source.

Note the architectural tension the playbook inherits by citing this at all: this source's whole
argument is **for RAG**, and the playbook uses its framing to motivate a chapter arguing RAG is the
wrong choice for mid-market. Kukreja's "activation not creation" insight is architecture-neutral —
it works for a compiled wiki too — but the playbook takes the framing and discards the conclusion
without saying so.
