## Body

The playbook's foundational adoption source, supplying Sections 1.1, 1.3, 1.4, and 3.3.

Adoption: "39% of SMEs now use AI applications (up from 26% in 2024)"; "26% specifically use
generative AI"; "Only 8% of businesses have reached 'transformative' digital integration".

Barriers: maintenance costs 40%, lack of training time 39%, hardware 32% — with management
resistance at 7% and employee resistance at 6%.

Security: "72% have inadequate digital security measures"; "32% of SMEs experienced a security
breach in the past year".

Outcomes among generative AI users: "91% reported significant productivity improvements"; "76%
experienced enhanced innovation capabilities".

Its own conclusion — that custom, tailored solutions beat generic off-the-shelf products for SMEs —
is the argument the playbook develops in Section 3.3.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**This is a vendor blog post summarizing OECD research, not the research.** Daijobu AI sells custom
AI solutions, and the article's conclusion is that SMEs need custom AI solutions. The OECD D4SME
survey has no URL, no direct citation, and no capture anywhere in the playbook — despite Appendix B
recommending it as a primary source.

The 6–7% resistance figures are self-reported by firms and contradicted by every change-management
source in this vault. See [[adoption-is-a-leadership-problem|Adoption Is a Leadership Problem]].

The playbook also attaches the "72% inadequate digital security / 32% breached" figures to its
Section 1.2 sentence about a "capability-ambition gap" credited to an academic paper — the figures
are from here, the concept is from nowhere. See [[capability-ambition-gap|Capability-Ambition Gap]].

The "€15.7 trillion by 2030" macro figure is a well-travelled PwC number, uncited here.
