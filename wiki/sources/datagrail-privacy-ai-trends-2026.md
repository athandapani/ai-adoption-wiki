## Body

- **"63.6% of AI-powered vendors did not disclose third-party AI subprocessors in their legal
  documentation"**
- "32.8% of AI systems also participate in at least one high-risk activity"
- "63% of websites still fail to honor GPC and other universal opt-out mechanisms"
- "DSR deletion requests have surged 567% since 2021"; "87% of all DSRs are deletion requests"
- "$1.5 million per year" for mid-sized organizations handling requests manually

The 63.6% figure is the basis for [[four-pillar-governance|Pillar 3: Vendor Oversight]] and,
independently corroborated by ref [^21], is one of the better-supported numbers in the governance
material.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**Appendix B misdescribes this source.** It lists DataGrail as the "Primary source for shadow AI
statistics". It isn't — it's the primary source for *subprocessor* statistics. The shadow AI numbers
(18k prompts, 223 violations, +$670K) come from Netskope and IBM via ref [^26]. The playbook's
in-text citations get this right; its own ingestion queue gets it wrong.

DataGrail sells privacy automation, and the report concludes privacy teams need privacy automation.
The "$1.5M/year manual handling" figure is directly self-serving.

Read via a **press release**, not the report — so methodology, sample size, and how "AI-powered
vendors" were selected are all unknown. A 63.6% figure implies a specific denominator that isn't
disclosed anywhere in the capture.
