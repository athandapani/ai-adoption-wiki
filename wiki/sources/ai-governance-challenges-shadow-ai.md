## Body

The enforcement timeline, verbatim:

> "Prohibited practices took effect in **February 2025**, and high-risk system requirements became
> enforceable in **August 2026**, carrying fines of up to **€35 million or 7% of global annual
> revenue**."

Shadow AI figures:
- "**78% of employees** admit to using AI tools not provided by their employer"
- "**45.4%** of sensitive data prompts submitted to large language model platforms originate from
  personal email accounts"
- "62% of confirmed incidents involve a human element" (Verizon 2026)

Its diagnosis — a **"governance velocity problem"**: AI adoption outpaces both legislative processes
and internal control maturity, so policies stay unenforced without continuous visibility.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**The playbook cites this source and omits its two most important numbers.** Section 4.1 says only
that high-risk requirements are "phasing in through 2026" — no August date, no €35M, no 7% of global
revenue. For a $200M mid-market firm, 7% is $14M. That is the fact that funds a governance
programme, and it's sitting unused in a source the playbook read.

The 78%-of-employees figure also sits oddly against ref [^26]'s **47%** on personal accounts. Both
are in this vault, describing overlapping behavior with a 31-point spread and no shared definition.

Adaptive Security sells human risk management and behavioral monitoring, and the article concludes
that human risk management and behavioral monitoring are prerequisites for governance.
