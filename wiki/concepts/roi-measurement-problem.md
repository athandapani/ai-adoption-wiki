## Body

BCG's finding, via Bob Marsh: **94% say they'll continue AI spending at current levels or higher
even if this year's initiatives don't deliver a return** — set against **6%** seeing meaningful
bottom-line impact.

The playbook reads this as "both confidence in the technology and a troubling absence of
measurement discipline". Fair. Lindsey Tishgart's data sharpens it considerably: "less than 1% of
executives report ROI of 20% or greater; 53% report only 1-5%"; "30% of GenAI projects abandoned
after POC"; "60% see minimal or no value"; and, most damaging to any productivity model, **"nearly
40% of AI time savings are lost to rework"**.

The playbook's prescription is the useful part, and it's the one thing here nobody else says:
**measure before you deploy.** Three baseline categories:

1. **Time metrics** — hours/week searching for information, resolving knowledge questions,
   onboarding new hires
2. **Error metrics** — rework rates, compliance incidents, decision reversals traceable to
   information gaps
3. **Attrition-linked knowledge loss** — documented cases where senior departures created
   operational gaps

Without a pre-AI baseline, post-AI ROI is unfalsifiable — which is precisely how you end up in the
94%.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

The 94% figure may be less irrational than it looks. If AI is a general-purpose technology, early
ROI measurement is genuinely misleading, and "we'll keep investing regardless" is a defensible bet
on a learning curve rather than a failure of discipline. Tishgart's own advice — build AI financial
governance with outcome-based metrics — assumes measurability that a 2-year-old capability may not
have yet.

The playbook doesn't notice that this section undercuts its own Section 2.4. It criticizes firms
for poor measurement discipline, then builds its Second Brain case on 312% and 300–500% ROI figures
from sources with no disclosed methodology, an untraceable $300K anchor, and the larger of two
conflicting search-time numbers. The measurement critique applies to the document making it.
