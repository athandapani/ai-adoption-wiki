## Body

From OECD's 2025 D4SME Survey:

| Barrier | % of SMEs citing it |
|---|---|
| Maintenance costs of AI systems | 40% |
| Lack of time for employee training | 39% |
| Hardware / infrastructure costs | 32% |
| Understanding digital regulations | 26% |
| Training and skills development costs | 24% |
| Management resistance | 7% |
| Employee resistance | 6% |

The playbook's reading: "the barriers are structural and resource-based, not attitudinal. This
shifts the intervention model from persuasion campaigns to **resource reallocation and
build-buy-partner decisions**."

**A second ranking, from a second source, disagrees.** Creative Genius surveyed 312 SMBs and got a
different order entirely: "Don't know which use case" (51%), "No internal AI talent" (44%), data
security (38%), ROI evaluation difficulty (35%), failed pilots (22%), **budget (17%)**. Cost ranks
*last* here and *first* in OECD. Both are in the playbook; only OECD's ordering informs its
conclusion.

The likely reconciliation: these surveys ask different populations at different stages. OECD asks
SMEs broadly — including the majority who haven't started, for whom cost is the salient
abstraction. Creative Genius asks operators who have bought something, for whom the binding
constraint turns out to be knowing what to point it at.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

The 6–7% resistance figures are the most consequential and least reliable numbers here. They are
**self-reported by the firm**, typically by an owner or IT lead — the same people whose own
resistance would be measured. Ajay Pundhir's finding that 76% of executives believe employees are
enthusiastic while only 31% are is precisely the bias that would drive resistance to the bottom of
a self-reported ranking.

Set beside Alice Labs' "82% of AI adoption failures trace to change management gaps" and Writer's
"31% of employees admit to actively undermining their company's AI rollout", the 6% figure looks
less like a finding about reality and more like a finding about who was asked.

Barrier-to-adoption and cause-of-failure are also simply different questions. Cost stops you
starting; people stop you finishing. The playbook uses an answer to the first to design an
intervention for the second.
