## Body

This page exists to record a claim that did not survive verification.

Playbook Section 1.2 states:

> "A 2025 academic study characterizes the mid-market AI challenge as a 'capability-ambition gap':
> the business case for AI is clear, but the organizational readiness — data quality, digital
> security, skills — consistently falls short."

The cited study is Schwaeke, Peters, Kanbach, Kraus & Jones, *The new normal: The status quo of AI
adoption in SMEs*, Journal of Small Business Management (DOI 10.1080/00472778.2024.2379999).

Checked against the paper's **full 108,008-character text**:

| String | Occurrences |
|---|---|
| "capability-ambition" | **0** |
| "ambition" (any form) | **0** |
| "digital security" | **0** |

The paper is a **systematic literature review of 106 peer-reviewed articles**, organized by the
technology–organization–environment model into eight clusters: compatibility, infrastructure,
knowledge, resources, culture, competition, regulation, ecosystem. It characterizes nothing as a
capability-ambition gap. It is also about **SMEs**, not the $50M–$500M mid-market band the
playbook applies it to.

The statistics attached to the sentence — "72% of mid-sized businesses have inadequate digital
security" and "32% experienced a security breach" — come from ref [^1] (OECD via Daijobu), where
they describe SMEs generally, and are correctly reported there.

**Why keep the page.** The underlying observation is reasonable and survives independently: mid-market
organizational readiness lags the business case. That's supported by OECD's barrier data and RSM's
"54% found deployment harder than anticipated". What doesn't survive is the attribution — a
quoted, named framework credited to a real paper that doesn't contain it. Deleting the page would
lose the finding; asserting the concept would launder it.

Status is `revisiting` rather than `dormant` because the idea isn't superseded — it was never
sourced.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

The fair defense: "capability-ambition gap" could be an editorial paraphrase of the paper's TOE
clusters (resources, knowledge, infrastructure *are* readiness dimensions) rather than a claimed
quotation. Coining a phrase to summarize a source isn't fabrication.

But the playbook puts it in quotation marks and says the study "characterizes" the challenge that
way. That's a claim about what the paper says, not a summary of it — and it's the form of claim a
reader would reasonably rely on without checking.

The verification here is a negative result over a retrieved full text, which is strong but not
absolute: the Playwright capture rendered the HTML full-text view, and a phrase appearing only in
a figure image, supplementary file, or the PDF typesetting would not be caught. Given "ambition"
appears zero times in 108k characters, this is unlikely.
