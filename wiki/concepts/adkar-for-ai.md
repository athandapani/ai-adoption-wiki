## Body

Prosci's ADKAR maps onto AI adoption. **Ref [^27]'s original table** (the playbook's version is a
rewrite — see below):

| Stage | In AI context | Common stall | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Awareness** | Understanding why AI is introduced and what problem it solves | Low comprehension causes early disengagement | Hallway questions about job risk |
| **Desire** | Individual can answer WIIFM for their specific role | Broadcast comms miss individual motivations | **High shadow-AI usage** |
| **Knowledge** | People know prompting, output limits, when to trust | Most workers receive zero employer AI training | Usage spikes then collapses |
| **Ability** | Real workflow application under normal pressure | Tool works in demos, fails in practice | Workarounds and manual fallbacks |
| **Reinforcement** | Use sustained beyond launch | Adoption treated as a project with an end date | Usage drifts down post-rollout |

**The most useful line in the table is the Desire warning sign**, and the playbook drops it. High
shadow-AI usage means people want AI badly enough to route around you — that's not an absence of
desire, it's desire the sanctioned path failed to capture. It connects
[[shadow-ai|Section 4.5's security material]] to
[[adoption-is-a-leadership-problem|Section 8's change material]], which the playbook otherwise
keeps in separate silos.

The tactics that follow: lead with the business problem, not the tool; name what stays human;
role-specific WIIFM; prompt libraries and office hours; embed in existing workflow; pair with
champions; **measure adoption, not deployment**.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

The playbook's Section 8.2 table is **not** ref [^27]'s table. It substitutes its own stall points
and warning signs, and its Desire row recommends segmenting by "employee regulatory focus —
prevention-focused staff need safety narratives, not opportunity narratives". That idea comes from
ref [^35] (Alice Labs), not ref [^27], and the playbook cites only [^27][^35] jointly without
distinguishing.

ADKAR itself is a proprietary consulting framework with limited independent validation. Its
sequential structure suits deliberate rollouts and fits AI badly: AI adoption is often
employee-initiated and *precedes* organizational awareness. When 47% of users are already on
personal accounts, "Awareness → Desire" describes a sequence that has already run backwards.
