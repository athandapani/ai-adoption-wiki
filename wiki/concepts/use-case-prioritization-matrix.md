## Body

| Quadrant | Action |
|---|---|
| High impact + high feasibility | **Quick wins** — deliver first to build credibility |
| High impact + low feasibility | **Strategic priorities** — begin groundwork, don't promise timelines |
| Low impact + high feasibility | **Traps** — technically easy, minimal return; decline politely |
| Low impact + low feasibility | **Reject** — use as an education opportunity on AI's real capabilities |

The **traps** quadrant is the one that matters. Nothing stops a 3–5 person team faster than a
backlog of easy, visible, low-value requests — each individually reasonable, collectively fatal to
the portfolio. The framework's real function is giving a lean team permission to say no.

This matters more than the playbook lets on: Creative Genius found **"Don't know which use case"
is the single largest adoption blocker at 51%** — ahead of talent (44%), security (38%), and budget
(17%). Use case selection isn't a governance formality; it's the binding constraint on SMB AI
adoption, and this matrix is the playbook's only answer to it.

Metrics that demonstrate CoE value to sponsors: business outcomes directly attributable to AI
(revenue uplift, cost reduction, risk incidents avoided); time from intake to production; and AI
literacy scores across the organization.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

Two-by-twos are easy to draw and hard to populate. Both axes are the things you don't know yet:
"business impact" is the ROI estimate that [[roi-measurement-problem|nobody can produce reliably]],
and "organizational feasibility" is the readiness assessment that 54% of RSM's respondents got
wrong ("deployment harder than anticipated"). Placing a use case on this grid requires the
judgment the grid was supposed to supply.

The framework also assumes impact and feasibility are independent. In practice they correlate
inversely and for the same reason — the highest-impact use cases touch the messiest data and the
most defended processes.

Ref [^24]'s original phrasing for the fourth quadrant is "reject without analysis", which is
sharper than the playbook's softened "use as education opportunity". No validation data exists for
any of it.
