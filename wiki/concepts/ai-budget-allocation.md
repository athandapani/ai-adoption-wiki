## Body

Creative Genius, 312 SMBs, 2026:

| Category | % of AI budget |
|---|---|
| Customer service / support deflection | 29% |
| Sales automation / SDR | 19% |
| Content production | 14% |
| Back-office automation (AP, document extraction) | 13% |
| Voice agents | 9% |
| **Internal knowledge / RAG / Second Brain** | **8%** |
| Marketing personalization | 5% |
| Other | 3% |

This is the single best-sourced dataset in the playbook's spend material: a stratified sample
matched to US Census SMB-by-industry distribution, 312 complete responses from a 4,800-operator
panel, disclosed incentive, **no vendor sponsorship**.

The playbook's reading — that 8% represents systematic under-investment given knowledge work's
share of payroll — is the argument developed at
[[internal-knowledge-is-under-invested|Internal Knowledge Is Systematically Under-Invested]].

Worth noting what buyers report getting: "Reduced costs" 72%, "Increased revenue per employee"
58%, "Improved customer satisfaction" 47% — but also **"No measurable impact" 18%** and **"Negative
impact" 4%**.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

The allocation pattern may be rational rather than mistaken. Support deflection produces a
measurable number next quarter; knowledge ROI is diffuse and lagging. Buyers may be pricing
attribution certainty, not missing value.

The playbook draws its under-investment conclusion by pairing this 8% with the 312% and 141% ROI
figures — the weakest citations in the corpus — and citing "141% over 3 years" from a source (ref
[^30]) that measures **RAG** knowledge bases, i.e. the architecture Section 2.3 argues against.

This is also a **single-source** table. No other survey in the corpus reports category allocation,
so there's nothing to triangulate against. The categories themselves are the survey author's, and
"internal knowledge / RAG / Second Brain" is a construct that may not map to how respondents
actually budget.
