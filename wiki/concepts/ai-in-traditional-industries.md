## Body

**Why these industries are different:** high tribal knowledge concentration (expertise in people's
heads, not systems); ERP-centric legacy estates (SAP, Oracle, Dynamics) with limited API access;
operational continuity requirements (you can't experiment when downtime has physical
consequences); workforce digital literacy gaps on the shop floor; and regulatory/safety constraints
that restrict "move fast".

**Manufacturing.** Use cases: predictive maintenance, computer-vision quality control, demand
forecasting, digital-twin process optimization. **44% of manufacturing decision-makers** cite
hallucination-driven accuracy concerns vs. a 36% cross-industry average — which is why
citation-first, RAG-grounded systems matter more here.
*Recommended first Second Brain:* maintenance procedure knowledge base — equipment manuals,
maintenance logs, past repair decisions — reducing mean time to repair and preserving expertise
from retiring technicians.

**Logistics and distribution.** **70% of transportation and logistics companies have adopted AI**,
one of the highest-adoption traditional sectors. Use cases: route optimization, demand sensing,
document automation (bills of lading, customs, proof-of-delivery), driver/fleet regulatory
knowledge.
*Recommended first Second Brain:* carrier and customer knowledge base — contract terms, SLA
history, issue resolutions, route knowledge — for dispatchers and account managers.

**Professional services.** Highest density of expert knowledge per capita, most to lose from
attrition. Use cases: client/matter knowledge bases, regulatory Q&A grounded in current text,
proposal reuse, systematic expert knowledge capture before senior staff retire.

**Healthcare services (non-clinical).** Billing, coding, scheduling, HR, supply chain, compliance —
rich AI application with lower regulatory friction than clinical AI. Use cases: prior authorization
drafting, compliance policy Q&A, staff onboarding, vendor/contract knowledge graphs.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**The manufacturing downtime figure is unsourced.** "Documented 20–40% reduction in unplanned
downtime" from predictive maintenance does not appear in ref [^34], the only source cited. That
source's actual manufacturing figures are different: 70% reduction in assembly failures, 50% cut in
QC effort, 80% improvement in visual inspection accuracy — none of them about downtime.

**The per-employee spend figures for these sectors are wrong** — see
[[ai-spending-by-industry-2026|AI Spending by Industry 2026]]. Manufacturing is $900/employee, not
the $1,800 the playbook claims; logistics has no figure in the source at all. The Section 6.5
"$360K–$700K starting budget" for a 200-person traditional-industry firm is built on both.

**Healthcare's section has no citations whatsoever.** Four use cases, zero references.

**The recommended Second Brain use cases are the playbook's own inventions** — plausible, unsourced,
and untested. No source documents a manufacturing maintenance wiki or a carrier knowledge base in
production.
