## Body

Governance isn't bureaucratic overhead — it's what prevents the three common mid-market AI failure
modes: **shadow AI data breaches**, **regulatory non-compliance**, and **hallucination-driven
business risk**.

The regulatory facts are not optional:

- **145 AI-related laws** enacted by US states in 2025, with 1,000+ bills introduced or revised
- **EU AI Act**: prohibited practices in force since February 2025; **high-risk system requirements
  enforceable from August 2026**, carrying fines up to **€35 million or 7% of global annual
  revenue**
- **California's 2028 attestation requirement** introduces **personal criminal liability** for
  executives signing inaccurate privacy assessments
- **42% of companies abandoned AI projects in 2025 due to privacy concerns**

Mid-market firms operating across jurisdictions carry obligations they often don't know they have,
with no one whose job it is to know.

The right question, as the playbook frames it: not "do we need governance?" but **"what is the
minimum viable governance structure that enables AI without creating liability?"**

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

The playbook understates its own case. It says high-risk requirements are "phasing in through
2026" and never mentions the **€35M / 7% penalty** or California's **personal criminal liability**
— both of which are in ref [^22] and ref [^21], which it cites. These are the facts that would
actually move a mid-market board, and they're missing.

The counter-argument worth weighing: most mid-market AI use is not high-risk under the AI Act.
Internal knowledge search, meeting summarization, and content drafting fall largely outside Annex
III. A firm that builds ISO 42001-grade governance for a Notion AI deployment has bought compliance
theatre — and per [[governance-enables-rather-than-polices|the over-governance argument]], driven
its people to personal ChatGPT accounts, creating the exact risk it was avoiding.

Also unaddressed: 145 state laws is a count, not an exposure assessment. No source here helps a
firm work out which of the 145 apply to it.
