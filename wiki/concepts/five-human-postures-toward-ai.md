## Body

**The WEF's actual five archetypes** (Timis & Khilji, 1 June 2026 — from sentiment analysis of
public social media plus cross-industry interviews):

1. **AI enthusiasts** — driven by innovation and efficiency; channel as champions/super-users
2. **AI curious** — intrigued but pragmatic; withhold buy-in until technical questions are
   answered. Bridge with low-stakes sandbox hackathons
3. **AI cautious** — focused on **data security, ethical guardrails, and harm to human
   collaboration**. Address with mandated human-in-the-loop protocols
4. **AI sceptics** — intellectual pragmatists who **doubt corporate hype and demand empirical
   proof**. Address with transparent A/B testing
5. **AI opposed** — driven by **job displacement and surveillance fears**. Address underlying
   anxieties directly, e.g. a formal "Pro-Human AI Charter"

**The playbook's version renames all five and mismaps two:**

| WEF | Playbook | Faithful? |
|---|---|---|
| AI enthusiasts | Enthusiasts | ✅ |
| AI curious | Pragmatists | ~ renamed |
| AI cautious *(security/ethics)* | **Skeptics** *("doubt AI's reliability")* | ❌ |
| AI sceptics *(demand proof)* | **Anxious** *("fear job displacement")* | ❌ |
| AI opposed *(displacement fears)* | Resisters | ~ partial |

The playbook's "Anxious" is the WEF's **opposed**. Its "Skeptics" is the WEF's **sceptics**, not
its cautious. So its per-posture interventions land on the wrong archetypes — and the **cautious**
category (data security and ethics) disappears entirely, which is the one most relevant to a
document with a whole section on shadow AI.

**What the playbook drops, and shouldn't have: frontstage/backstage resistance.** The WEF's actual
finding is that employees "appear to comply with AI mandates to manage impressions" in public while
"clowning" the tools and circumventing them in private — and that **even enthusiasts privately doubt
effectiveness**. That is the study's contribution, and it's the direct mechanism behind
[[shadow-ai|shadow AI]]: 47% on personal accounts is backstage resistance with a network signature.

The one figure the playbook adds that earns its place: PwC's **~62% wage premium** for AI-skilled
workers (ref [^27], "up from 57% the prior year") — the datapoint most likely to convert anxiety
into motion.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

Archetype taxonomies are seductive and weakly predictive. People move between postures by context —
enthusiastic about drafting, opposed to performance review. Ajay Pundhir's alternative cut is more
actionable because it classifies the **resistance** rather than the person: tool resistance,
strategy resistance, trust resistance, capability resistance. Each names a fix; "you have four
anxious people" does not.

The WEF's method — sentiment analysis of public social media plus interviews — measures what people
*say publicly*, which is precisely the frontstage the study warns is unreliable.

Status `revisiting`: the underlying research is sound, the playbook's rendering of it is not.
