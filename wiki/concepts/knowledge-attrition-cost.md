## Body

The playbook's financial anchor, Section 2.4:

> "organizations lose approximately **$300,000 in unrecorded knowledge with each departing senior
> employee**. For mid-market companies with moderate senior staff turnover, knowledge preservation
> alone can justify a Second Brain investment within the first year."

Section 7.4 applies it again to professional services, where knowledge density per capita is
highest.

This is the single most decision-relevant number in the document. If it holds, a firm losing three
senior people a year is bleeding ~$900K, and almost any knowledge investment pays for itself.

**It is attributed to ref [^10] (Stratechi). It does not appear in the capture of ref [^10].**

What Stratechi *does* say, and what checks out: organizations retain approximately "70% of
previously ephemeral knowledge" from departing senior employees with RAG systems; knowledge workers
spend "2.5 hours daily" searching; productivity gains of "30-50%".

The 70%-preservation figure is real. The $300,000 it's multiplied against is not sourced.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

The concept is sound even if the number isn't. Knowledge does leave with people, and it is
expensive — the replacement-cost literature on senior technical staff routinely lands in six
figures once ramp time is counted. The playbook may have imported a real figure from an uncited
source, or generated a plausible one. From the reference list alone there's no way to tell.

But the specific number is doing specific work. "$300,000" and "within the first year" together
produce a payback claim precise enough to take to a CFO. A figure that precise needs a method: is
it replacement cost? Lost productivity during ramp? Foregone revenue? Each yields a different
number and a different argument.

Related unverified figure: the "$1M–$2M annually" recovered-capacity estimate for a 100-person
organization rests on the 2.5-hours-daily search figure — while another source the playbook cites
(ref [^40], AIpedia) says **1.8 hours/day**. The playbook uses the larger figure and doesn't
mention the smaller.

What would change this: a primary source for the $300,000 figure with a stated method. Until then
`revisiting`, and don't put it in a board deck.
