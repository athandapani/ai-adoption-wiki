## Body

The gap is not adoption. It's transformation depth.

OECD reports **only 8% of businesses** have reached "transformative" digital integration, where
AI genuinely reshapes how work is done; 16% remain at basic digital adoption. BCG's AI Radar 2026
finds **only 6% of companies** using AI see meaningful bottom-line impact.

Most firms sit at the tool-adoption phase — buying subscriptions, running pilots — without the
architectural or process changes that compound value. The playbook's diagnosis is that this is
where mid-market gets stuck by default, because moving past it requires exactly the capabilities
(architects, platform teams) mid-market doesn't have.

Two corroborating figures from elsewhere in the corpus sharpen the picture. Mavvrik: "less than
1% of executives report ROI of 20% or greater; 53% report only 1-5%", and "30% of GenAI projects
abandoned after POC". SID Global Solutions: "Nearly 70% of enterprises reported that 30% or fewer
of their GenAI pilots made it to production".

Every independent measure of *value* clusters low, while every measure of *usage* clusters high.
That's the gap.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

"Transformative" is undefined in the OECD framing, and 8% is exactly the kind of number that
depends entirely on where a survey designer drew the line. The same is true of BCG's "meaningful
bottom-line impact".

A more interesting objection: the gap may be a timing artifact rather than a failure. Enterprise
technology historically shows a long lag between adoption and measured productivity — the Solow
paradox. Six percent seeing impact in year two of a general-purpose technology may be unremarkable
rather than alarming. Aitoolguide's own guidance says maturity takes 6–12 months after a 4–12 week
integration; a 2026 survey is measuring deployments mostly younger than that.

The playbook treats the gap as evidence for its architectural prescription. It is equally
consistent with "it's early".
