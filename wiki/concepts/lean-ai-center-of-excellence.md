## Body

The traditional CoE — 30+ data scientists, dedicated platform teams, a governance function — is
over-engineered for mid-market and produces exactly what sponsors most want to avoid: nothing
shipped.

**Five components** (ref [^24], reproduced faithfully by the playbook):

1. **Executive sponsorship** — one named sponsor with budget authority and willingness to clear
   organizational blockers
2. **A team of 3–5 people** with complementary skills (domain expertise + technical fluency +
   change management)
3. **Three use cases** to start — "enough to build a portfolio, few enough to execute well"
4. **A clear governance framework** — the [[four-pillar-governance|four pillars]]
5. **An external partner relationship** to fill expertise gaps without permanent headcount

**Time allocation — the counter-intuitive part:**

- **40%** governance
- **30%** internal education and AI literacy
- **30%** direct delivery

Most sponsors expect the inverse. Resisting that pressure is the point: CoEs that skip governance
and literacy produce short-term demos and long-term liability.

**Roles** (playbook's Section 5.3 — note this table is *not* in ref [^24] and appears to be the
playbook's own):

| Role | Can be combined with |
|---|---|
| AI Sponsor (Executive) | Existing CDO / CTO / COO |
| AI Program Lead | Digital transformation lead |
| AI Engineer / Architect | Platform engineer or senior developer |
| AI Champion Network | Business analysts, team leads |
| External AI Partner | Consulting engagement, not FTE |

The **AI Champion Network** is the highest-leverage element for adoption at scale — peer advocates
per function build trust in a way central IT cannot. Ref [^27] backs this: peer champions answer
the practical "how do I" questions broadcast communications can't, and Microsoft reached "~90%
monthly active usage" through peer-to-peer learning rather than mandates.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

The playbook omits the one thing that makes the model work: **an end date**. Amit Kothari's version
— which the playbook cites in Section 11.1 — specifies an **18-month timeline with "an end date
from day one"**. Without it, "make the CoE invisible over time" is an aspiration every permanent
bureaucracy has always had. See [[ai-coe-should-work-itself-out-of-a-job|the dissolution argument]].

The 40/30/30 split has no derivation and no validation. It's a consultancy's recommendation stated
as a finding.

Real tension: a 3–5 person team spending 40% on governance is ~2 FTE of governance for an
organization that by assumption has no compliance function. That's either too little to satisfy
ISO 42001 or too much to ship three use cases — and no source addresses which.
