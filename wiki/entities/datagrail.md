## Body

DataGrail's **2026 Privacy and AI Trends Report** (27 May 2026):

- **"63.6% of AI-powered vendors did not disclose third-party AI subprocessors in their legal
  documentation"**
- "32.8% of AI systems also participate in at least one high-risk activity"
- "63% of websites still fail to honor GPC and other universal opt-out mechanisms"
- "DSR deletion requests have surged 567% since 2021"; "87% of all DSRs are deletion requests"
- "$1.5 million per year" for mid-sized organizations managing requests manually

The 63.6% figure is the evidentiary basis for [[four-pillar-governance|Pillar 3: Vendor Oversight]]
and, more importantly, for the *vendor-embedded* half of the shadow AI problem — AI reaching your
data through tools you already approved. The same figure appears independently in ref [^21], which
draws the sharper conclusion.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**DataGrail sells privacy automation software**, and the report's conclusion is that privacy
programs must adopt privacy automation software. The $1.5M manual-handling cost figure is a
directly self-serving number.

The playbook cites this report for the 63.6% figure and, correctly, not for its Section 4.5
shadow-AI statistics — those come from ref [^26]. Worth stating because Appendix B lists DataGrail
as the "Primary source for shadow AI statistics", which is wrong: it's the primary source for
*subprocessor* statistics. Netskope and IBM supply the shadow AI numbers.

Read via a GlobeNewswire press release rather than the report itself, so methodology and sample are
unknown.
