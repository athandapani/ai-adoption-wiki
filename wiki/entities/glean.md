## Body

**Category:** Enterprise AI search.
**Price:** $40–100/user/yr ($100K–2M total).
**Differentiator:** "Universal Search (100+ connectors)" plus AI Assistant and Agents — cross-system
unified search.
**Best fit:** 500+ employee organizations with knowledge fragmented across many systems.

Glean appears in the recommended stack for mid-market firms at $100M–$300M ARR with 100–500
employees ("Glean + Notion + Guru, ~$150K/year") — the profile where knowledge is scattered across
CRM, docs, and communications and the connector count is the actual product.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

The pricing spread is the tell: $100K–$2M is not a price, it's a sales process. For a mid-market
firm at the bottom of the playbook's band ($50M ARR, ~$850K total AI budget at BCG's 1.7%
benchmark), Glean alone could consume a fifth of the AI budget before any use case ships.

Glean is also the tool most directly opposed to the playbook's own thesis. It is **search over raw
sources across systems** — precisely the "RAG retrieves" architecture Section 2.3 argues degrades
past a few hundred documents. The playbook recommends it in Section 10.2 anyway, for exactly the
organizations Section 2.3 says it fails. Neither section acknowledges the other.

Both tool sources ([^40], [^41]) are affiliate-style comparison sites; neither discloses commercial
relationships.
