## Body

RSM's **2025 Middle Market AI Survey (US and Canada)**, via ref [^2], is the only source in the
corpus that measures the playbook's actual target population rather than "SMEs" generally:

- **78–92% of mid-sized firms** are using AI
- **85%** report AI has exceeded expectations
- **54%** found deployment harder than anticipated
- **89%** plan to increase AI budgets; 74% focusing on generative AI
- **67%** need external assistance for strategy and governance

RSM is also investing "$1 billion over three years" in AI agents, infrastructure, and client-facing
tools, reporting efficiency gains "reaching 80% in compliance and audit functions".

The 85%-exceeded / 54%-harder pairing is the survey's most useful result: satisfaction and
difficulty are both high, which is the [[mid-market-ai-paradox|paradox]] in two numbers.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**The 78–92% adoption figure is irreconcilable with the rest of the corpus.** OECD says 39% of
SMEs; Alice Labs says 18% of firms use AI in at least one business function as of January 2026.
The playbook stacks these without noting the range spans 4×. Definitions of "using AI" almost
certainly differ — a licensed seat vs. a production workflow — but no source states its definition.

**The 67%-need-external-help figure is published by a firm that sells external help**, in a survey
whose coverage doubles as an announcement of its own $1B AI investment. That doesn't make it
false; it makes it a marketing artifact.

Read via a LinkedIn post summarizing the survey; the survey itself was never fetched and has no URL
in the playbook.
