## Body

WEF's *The 5 faces of human readiness for AI adoption* (David Timis and Shaista Khilji, 1 June
2026, part of the Centre for AI Excellence) is the source of the playbook's Section 8.4.

Its five archetypes: **AI enthusiasts, AI curious, AI cautious, AI sceptics, AI opposed** — derived
from "a two-phase study combining sentiment analysis of public social media spaces with in-depth
interviews across multiple industries".

Its actual contribution, which the playbook drops entirely, is **frontstage compliance / backstage
resistance**: employees "appear to comply with AI mandates to manage impressions" publicly, while
"clowning" tools and circumventing them privately — and **even enthusiasts "often privately doubt
their effectiveness"**.

Other figures: "62% of adults now interact with AI several times a week" (US); global private AI
investment "estimated at $660 billion in 2026"; the gap between AI capability and AI use is a
**"capability overhang"**.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

The playbook **renames all five archetypes and mismaps two** — see
[[five-human-postures-toward-ai|Five Human Postures Toward AI]]. It also dates the research to
"May 2026"; it was published 1 June 2026.

The study's method is its own weak point: sentiment analysis of **public social media** measures
precisely the frontstage the paper argues is unreliable. Interviews mitigate this but are not
described in enough detail to assess.

Note the irony worth preserving: this is the one source in the corpus that explains *why* the
playbook's other sources — self-reported barrier surveys like [[oecd|OECD]]'s 6% employee
resistance — should be distrusted. The playbook cites it and keeps the surveys.
