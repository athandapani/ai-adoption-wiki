## Body

Forrester's finding, via ref [^16]: **"only half offer training to non-technical employees, with
that figure growing just 4 percentage points between 2024 and 2025"**.

The 4-point figure is the sharp part. Organizations are deploying AI far faster than they are
enabling people to use it, and the enablement gap is barely closing — a year of intense AI
attention moved the number almost not at all.

Also via the same capture: "43 percent of employees believing many people will lose jobs to
automation within five years".

This corroborates [[ai-literacy|AI Literacy]]'s case from an independent direction: Victor Hoang
reports only 35% of organizations have a mature workforce-wide upskilling program, and Amit Kothari
reports two-thirds of workers saying their organization hasn't been proactive on AI training.
Three sources, same shape.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

Read via ref [^16], a news aggregator (mediastack) summarizing a Forrester report. The report isn't
named, dated, or linked, and Forrester research is paywalled — so the sample, population, and
question wording are all unknown.

"Offer training" is also a low bar that tells you nothing about uptake or quality. An org that
publishes an optional e-learning module counts as offering training.
