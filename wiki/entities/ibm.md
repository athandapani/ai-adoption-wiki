## Body

IBM's **2025 Cost of a Data Breach Report**, via ref [^26]:

- **"20% of organizations reported a breach due to security incidents involving Shadow AI"**
- **"High Shadow AI environments saw +$670,000 higher breach costs vs low/no Shadow AI"**
- **"Shadow AI incidents resulted in more PII (65%) and more IP (40%) being compromised"**

The +$670K premium is the number the playbook leans on hardest in Section 4.5, and reasonably so —
it converts a governance abstraction into a line item. It's also the figure that makes the
[[ninety-day-governance-launch|90-day plan]] pay for itself against
[[three-tier-sme-governance|$200/month Standard-tier governance]].

The **20%-of-organizations** figure is arguably more decision-relevant and the playbook omits it
entirely: it's a probability, not a severity, and it's what turns the $670K into an expected value.

IBM also appears in ref [^23]'s Premium governance tier ("OneTrust + IBM Watson", $1,500+/month).

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

Read second-hand via a LinkedIn post. The Cost of a Data Breach Report is public and free, and the
playbook's Appendix B recommends ingesting it — it wasn't.

The +$670K figure is a **correlation across environments**, not a causal effect of shadow AI.
Organizations with high shadow AI plausibly differ in many ways — weaker security culture, faster
growth, less mature IT — any of which would independently raise breach costs. The report measures
association; the playbook reads it as "shadow AI costs you $670K".

Note also that IBM sells AI governance tooling, and appears in this vault both as the source of the
risk statistic and as a vendor in the remediation tier.
