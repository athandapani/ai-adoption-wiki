## Body

Prosci's **ADKAR** is the standard individual-level change model: **Awareness, Desire, Knowledge,
Ability, Reinforcement**. The playbook adopts it wholesale as the sequencing spine for Section 8,
and Appendix A defines it as "the standard individual-level change model applied to AI adoption in
this report".

Its virtue for AI adoption is that it operates at the level of the individual rather than the
program — which matches what every change-management source in this vault reports: adoption
succeeds or fails person by person, not through rollout plans.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**Prosci is never cited.** It has no numbered reference and no URL in the playbook. Appendix B
recommends ingesting "Prosci ADKAR for AI (2026 change management studies)" as a primary source;
that was not done. ADKAR arrives entirely through refs [^27] and [^35], both marketing content from
firms selling change services.

ADKAR is also **proprietary consulting IP with limited independent validation**. It is widely used
because it is widely marketed; the peer-reviewed evidence base is thin relative to its ubiquity.

Its sequential structure fits AI badly. ADKAR assumes change is introduced *to* people —
awareness precedes desire precedes knowledge. AI adoption routinely runs backwards: employees adopt
first (47% on personal accounts), and the organization becomes aware afterward. Applying a
top-down sequence to a bottom-up phenomenon is why "high shadow-AI usage" shows up as a *Desire*
warning sign in ref [^27] — the model registers as a deficit what is actually excess demand.
