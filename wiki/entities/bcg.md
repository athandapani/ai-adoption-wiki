## Body

BCG's **AI Radar 2026** is the most-cited benchmark in the playbook's spend material:

- Companies plan to spend **~1.7% of annual revenues** on AI in 2026 — up from 0.8% in 2025
- **94%** will continue at that level or higher even without a return
- Only **6%** of companies using AI see meaningful bottom-line impact
- 88% of Indian leaders expect positive ROI vs. an 82% global average
- 36% of India's workforce is AI-skilled, below the 44% global average

The 1.7% figure is **corroborated across three independent captures** (refs [^4], [^28], [^31]),
which makes it one of the better-evidenced numbers in this vault. Ref [^31] independently supplies
the 2025 base of 0.8%, confirming the "more than double" claim.

BCG also appears via ref [^37] for a different finding: **76% of executives believe employees feel
enthusiastic about AI adoption; just 31% actually do** (BCG/Columbia Business School).

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**The playbook gets the report's name wrong.** It calls it "BCG Radar 6: AI Investments Surge, CEOs
Take the Lead (2026)" in Section 6.1 and Appendix B, and recommends ingesting it as a primary
source. The report is **AI Radar 2026**. "BCG Radar 6" appears in none of the three captured
sources that report its findings, and the report itself is never fetched — every BCG figure here is
third-party coverage.

**The sector split is unverified.** "Technology and financial institutions lead at ~2% of revenues;
industrial and real estate trail at ~0.8%" is cited to [^28][^29]. Ref [^29] was unreachable; ref
[^28] doesn't contain it.

**The 1.7% benchmark diverges sharply from observed behavior.** Creative Genius's actual median
spend at $100M–$200M revenue is $390,000 — roughly 4× below what 1.7% would imply. A survey of
stated 2026 plans and a survey of 2026 actuals disagree, and the playbook builds its budget
guidance on the former.
