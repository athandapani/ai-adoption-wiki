## Body

The US National Institute of Standards and Technology supplies the playbook's working definition of
an AI hallucination: **"confidently stated but false content"** (Appendix A, Section 9.1).

Ref [^25] also names the **NIST AI Risk Management Framework** alongside OECD principles as the
governance frameworks enterprises should adopt for hallucination risk.

The definition is doing real work in this vault. "Confidently stated" is the operative half — the
enterprise risk isn't that models are wrong, it's that they're wrong in the register of being
right, which is what defeats [[ai-literacy|output verification]] by untrained readers.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

NIST is never cited directly. The definition arrives via ref [^25], a vendor article, with no link
to a NIST publication. The AI RMF is real and public, so this is a verifiable claim that simply
wasn't verified — but the specific phrase "confidently stated but false content" is attributed
without a document, section, or date.

NIST's framework is also voluntary and US-centric. For a mid-market firm facing the
[[eu-ai-act|EU AI Act]]'s enforceable requirements and 145 US state laws, NIST AI RMF adoption is
useful posture and not a compliance answer.
