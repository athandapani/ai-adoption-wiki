## Body

The OECD's **2025 D4SME Survey** (Digital for SMEs) is the playbook's foundational adoption source,
supplying the barrier rankings in Section 1.3, the adoption rates in Section 1.1, and the
27%-not-adapted-to-their-needs figure in Section 3.3.

Key figures attributed to it: 39% of SMEs use AI applications (up from 26% in 2024); 26% use
generative AI; only 8% have reached "transformative" digital integration; maintenance costs (40%)
and lack of training time (39%) are the top barriers; management and employee resistance rank last
at 7% and 6%.

Also cited for data readiness: "access to high-quality, labeled, and structured data remains a
major challenge".

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

**The playbook never reads the OECD directly.** Every OECD figure arrives via ref [^1], a vendor
blog post (Daijobu AI) summarizing OECD research, or ref [^12], a G7/Mila PDF that could not be
captured. Appendix B recommends ingesting "OECD 'SME Digitalisation for Competitiveness' (2025
D4SME Survey)" as a primary source; that was never done, and the survey has no URL anywhere in the
playbook.

The 6–7% resistance figures — the ones driving the playbook's Section 1.3 conclusion that barriers
are "structural, not attitudinal" — are self-reported by firms, and are contradicted by every
change-management source in this vault. See
[[adoption-is-a-leadership-problem|Adoption Is a Leadership Problem]].

Ref [^17] also attributes to the OECD a claim that organizations with formal AI training achieve
"2.3x faster AI adoption and 67% higher AI ROI". That figure appears in no OECD capture here and is
unverified.
