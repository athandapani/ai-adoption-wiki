## Body

The allocation data is solid. Creative Genius surveyed 312 SMBs and found internal knowledge/RAG
receives **8%** of AI budget — behind customer service (29%), sales automation (19%), content
production (14%), and back-office automation (13%). That's a real, well-sourced finding from a
stratified sample with disclosed methodology and no vendor sponsorship.

The argument built on it is that 8% is too low given the returns. This is where it gets thin.

**The ROI evidence is weak in a specific way.** The headline figures — 300–500% first-year ROI
(Stratechi), 312% in six months (10xclaw), 141% over three years (Sverdlik) — come from a
consultancy marketing page, an anonymous case study with no author or date, and a LinkedIn post.
None discloses methodology. The 312% case describes a single 200-employee technology company;
the playbook generalizes it to manufacturing and distribution without comment.

**The anchor figure is untraceable.** "Organizations lose approximately $300,000 in unrecorded
knowledge with each departing senior employee" is the number that makes the investment case close
inside a year. It is attributed to Stratechi. It does not appear in the capture of Stratechi.

**The arithmetic uses the larger of two cited numbers.** The $1M–$2M annual recovered-capacity
estimate rests on knowledge workers spending 2.5 hours/day searching (Stratechi). Another source
the playbook cites — AIpedia, ref 40 — says 1.8 hours/day. The playbook uses 2.5 and never
mentions 1.8. That's a ~39% difference flowing straight into the headline.

**And a cited source contradicts the productivity math entirely.** Mavvrik reports "nearly 40% of
AI time savings are lost to rework" and "less than 1% of executives report ROI of 20% or greater;
53% report only 1-5%". The playbook cites Mavvrik in Section 6.4 for IT-budget share and omits
both figures.

So: the under-investment claim may well be true. The 8% allocation against knowledge work's share
of payroll is genuinely striking. But the case as the playbook argues it is built on the four
weakest citations in the corpus, and the strongest contrary evidence sits inside sources it
already cites.

## Counter-arguments & Data Gaps

The obvious counter: markets aren't obviously wrong. 312 SMBs independently put 29% into customer
service and 8% into internal knowledge. The deflection ROI on support automation is measurable
next quarter; knowledge ROI is diffuse, lagging, and hard to attribute. Buyers may be rationally
pricing certainty, not failing to see value. "Everyone else is under-investing in the thing I'm
selling" is the oldest argument in enterprise software.

There's also a survivorship problem. Documented RAG case studies are the ones that worked and got
written up by the people who built them. Mavvrik's "30% of GenAI projects abandoned after POC" is
the denominator, and no knowledge-system case study in this corpus reports a failure.

Status is `revisiting` deliberately: the allocation data is trustworthy, the returns argument
resting on it is not yet.

What would change this: a knowledge-system ROI study with disclosed methodology, a named author, a
control group, and a sample larger than one.
